Before you buy on the coast

The six things I walk every buyer through, in the order they come up.

  1. Find the flood zone

    X, AE or VE changes the insurance, the lender's rules and sometimes the resale. It's on every listing in my book.

  2. Ask for the elevation certificate

    It shows how high the living floor sits above base flood elevation. Higher can mean a much smaller flood premium.

  3. Get insurance quotes before the offer

    Flood and wind, from a local agent, during your due diligence, not the week before closing.

  4. Read the wind deductible

    Coastal policies often carry a separate percentage deductible for named storms. Know the dollar amount.

  5. Order the termite letter

    South Carolina closings use the CL-100 wood infestation report. Lenders ask for it, and so should you.

  6. Check sewer or septic, and the POA

    Many island homes are on septic. Gated communities have POA rules and dues; read both before you sign.

Questions buyers ask me

Is flood insurance required?

If the house is in a high-risk zone (A or V) and you have a federally backed mortgage, yes. In zone X it's optional, and often cheap enough to be worth it. I'll get you a quote either way.

Can I buy from out of state?

Yes. Video walkthroughs, a local inspector, and a closing attorney who can handle a remote signing. South Carolina closings are run by attorneys.

What does a coastal inspection add?

The usual home inspection, plus the CL-100 termite letter, a look at the pilings and the crawlspace on raised houses, and a septic inspection where there's one.

Do you work with sellers too?

Yes. I'll pull the flood paperwork together before we list, because buyers ask for it on the first showing.
