Walkthrough notes on agent websites

Things we see a lot. Common, not universal. Written up the way an inspector would.

  1. 01
    Observed

    The home page is a search bar.

    A search box with no listings behind it, or the same feed every agent in town has. Nothing says who you are.

    Recommend

    Lead with you and your areas. Search sits one tap down, for the people who want it.

  2. 02
    Observed

    No way to ask "what's my house worth?"

    The seller lead, the one worth the most, has nowhere to land.

    Recommend

    A home value request on the first screen, answered by you with a range from recent sales.

  3. 03
    Observed

    Neighborhood pages say "great schools, safe, family-friendly."

    That's the kind of wording fair housing guidance warns agents about.

    Recommend

    Write about the houses, streets, drive times and what's walkable. You approve every word.

  4. 04
    Observed

    The brokerage name is missing, or the license is.

    Most states want the brokerage clearly on agent advertising, and a website counts.

    Recommend

    Brokerage, license number and the Equal Housing statement in the footer and on the contact section.

  5. 05
    Observed

    The contact form texts people with no consent line.

    Then the follow-up text is the part that gets you in trouble.

    Recommend

    A plain consent sentence above the Send button, on every form.

  6. 06
    Observed

    Reviews are a screenshot from 2019.

    Nobody can click it, and Google can't read it.

    Recommend

    Your newest real reviews, with names and dates, linked to where they were posted.

Questions agents ask

My brokerage already gives me a website. Why pay for one?

Keep it. A brokerage page usually sits on the brokerage's domain, next to the rest of the roster, and stays there if you move. Yours is on your own domain with your name on it, and it follows you.

Can my site show MLS listings?

If your brokerage or an IDX provider gives you a search widget, we put it on the site. MLS data comes with rules from your MLS, so the feed itself has to come from them. We don't sell feeds.

What about fair housing in the neighborhood pages?

We write about houses, streets, drive times and what's nearby, not about who lives there, and we don't rate schools or safety. You read and approve every page before it goes up.

Will it have my brokerage and license on it?

Yes. Your brokerage name, license number and the Equal Housing Opportunity statement go in the footer and by the contact form. If your broker has a sign-off rule for ads, the preview is the thing to send them.

I'm on a team. Does it work for us?

Yes. It can be a team site with each agent's contact, or your own site that names the team. Tell us in the brief.

Who owns the domain if I switch brokerages?

You do. We register it in your name. When you move, text us and we change the brokerage line and logo.

How long does it take?

About 7 days from the day we get your headshot, areas, reviews and brokerage details.
