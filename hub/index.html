Cold email

Email is the main growth channel, run for deliverability first. Owners of local shops reply far less than office buyers, so volume comes from three trades and clean sending, not from blasting one list.

How it sends

  • Never from groundwork-web.com. Three separate sending domains with two named inboxes each, warmed for 3 to 4 weeks first.
  • 15 to 20 cold emails per inbox per day, about 2,000 to 2,500 a month once warm.
  • SPF, DKIM and DMARC on every domain. Open tracking off.

What it says

  • Under 80 words, plain text, one real fact about their shop and one easy question.
  • The ask is a free mockup of their site, not a price.
  • Four to six touches over about three weeks, then stop.
  • A real postal address and a one-word opt-out in every email.

Sequences written

  • Detailers with reviews and no site
  • Detailers whose site is losing jobs
  • Exterior cleaning
  • Landscaping and lawn care

Lead lists

Lists are built in-house rather than bought: cheaper, fresher, and inside the rules that scraped or bought lists break.

  1. Find the businesses through Google's official Places API: name, phone, website, rating.
  2. Read their own website politely for an address they publish. Robots.txt is honored, one page a second, five pages at most. Facebook, Yelp and similar platforms are never crawled.
  3. Verify every address and keep only the ones that pass. Addresses are never guessed.
  4. Split the output into an email list and a call list of shops with a phone, 5+ reviews and no real website.

Opt-outs, bounces and clients are suppressed before anything is verified or exported. Google's data is purged after 30 days, as its terms require. Contact data never enters this repository. The code is written and tested; the first sample run waits on an API key.

Mockups

The third email in each sequence links a free mockup of the shop's own site, built from its public facts. A script makes them at no extra cost per prospect.

What goes on a mockup

  • The shop's real name, town, phone, rating and up to three of its own Google reviews, with Google's attribution.
  • Prices, photos and service area marked "Sample".
  • A bar on top: "Mockup for this shop. Made by GroundWork. Not published." Never indexed.

What stops a mockup

  • Any number or claim that isn't one of the shop's facts.
  • An unlabelled sample section, an unfilled variable, or a first line over 240 characters.
  • A shop on the do-not-contact list.

A person reads a sample of every batch against the Google listings before it can be sent, and one wrong fact holds the batch. Mockups will render from the new demo designs, so none go out until those pass.

Design bar

Every site gets a "does this look generic?" check before it counts. One tell fails it outright; it needs 6 of 8 signs that a person made it for one business.

Fails on sight

  • A centered headline over a blurred photo with two pill buttons
  • Purple-to-blue gradients, glass cards, neon glows
  • A row of three icon cards
  • Copy that fits any business: "elevate", "seamless", "your trusted partner"
  • Empty boxes where photos go
  • Sample reviews that read like ad copy

Signs a person made it

  • One clear idea running through the site
  • Trade details only an insider would add
  • A deliberate type pairing with one typographic moment
  • Texture: a caption in the owner's voice, an asymmetric grid
  • The owner's own voice and local streets
  • A layout built for this buyer
  • One interaction that does a job, like a quote estimator
  • A palette from the trade's own world

Every site also needs a headline naming the service and town, call and booking within thumb reach, a 2-step quote form, a mobile speed score of 90+, full accessibility marks and structured data for local search. The builders work from a library of 37 vetted design, motion, copy and search skills kept in this repository; this bar overrides all of them.

Decisions

The calls that shape the work, all made on October 3, 2026.

  • Cold email is the main channel. Every choice is weighed against cost per client and deliverability.
  • Three trades, built in parallel. Detailing, exterior cleaning and landscaping, each with its own sequence and five sites.
  • One price list for all trades. Lawn care pitches annual first and has no winter pause.
  • Mockup links never point at the main domain, so outreach can't hurt groundwork-web.com's reputation.
  • No mockup goes out until it renders from a design that passed the design bar.
  • Real proof only. No invented reviews, ratings or results, ever.
  • Nothing is done below A+. Work that passed earlier went back for grading.
